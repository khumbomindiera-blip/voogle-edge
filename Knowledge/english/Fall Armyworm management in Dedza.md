# Fall Armyworm Management in Dedza District

## Summary

Fall Armyworm (FAW) is one of the most destructive pests affecting maize production in Dedza District and across Malawi. Effective management relies on Integrated Pest Management (IPM), which combines regular scouting, cultural practices, biological control, botanical pesticides, and responsible use of approved chemical pesticides.

---

# Identifying Fall Armyworm

Farmers should regularly inspect maize plants for signs of infestation.

Common signs include:

- Holes in young maize leaves
- Ragged or shredded leaves
- Caterpillars feeding inside the maize whorl
- Large amounts of insect droppings (frass) inside the plant
- Damaged growing points in young maize plants

Early detection allows timely control and reduces crop losses.

---

# Cultural and Preventive Practices

The following farming practices help reduce Fall Armyworm infestations.

## Plant Early

Plant maize immediately after the first effective rains.

Early planting allows crops to establish before Fall Armyworm populations become severe.

---

## Intercrop with Legumes

Intercrop maize with crops such as:

- Groundnuts
- Beans
- Soybeans
- Pigeon peas

Intercropping reduces pest damage and encourages beneficial insects.

---

## Keep Fields Clean

Maintain clean field borders by removing grass weeds and volunteer maize plants that may provide shelter for pests.

Protect trees and shrubs that provide habitat for beneficial insects that naturally feed on Fall Armyworm.

---

## Maintain Healthy Crops

Apply recommended fertilizer rates and maintain good crop nutrition.

Healthy maize plants recover more easily from minor pest damage.

---

# Monitoring and Scouting

Inspect maize fields every week beginning one week after crop emergence.

During scouting:

- Check the underside of leaves.
- Look inside the maize whorl.
- Inspect plants early in the morning or late in the afternoon.
- Record the number of affected plants.

Early scouting allows farmers to control the pest before severe damage occurs.

---

# Mechanical Control

In small gardens and household fields:

- Crush egg masses by hand.
- Remove young caterpillars from the maize whorl.
- Destroy heavily infested plant parts where practical.

These methods are effective when infestations are detected early.

---

# Botanical Control

Natural pesticides may help reduce Fall Armyworm populations.

A commonly used botanical option is:

## Tephrosia vogelii (Mthumba)

Crush fresh leaves.

Mix with clean water.

Filter the mixture before spraying.

Apply directly into the maize whorl where caterpillars are feeding.

Botanical pesticides should be applied regularly after rainfall if infestation continues.

---

# Chemical Control

Chemical pesticides should only be used when infestations are severe and other control methods are insufficient.

Approved active ingredients include:

- Emamectin Benzoate
- Flubendiamide

Always:

- Read and follow label instructions.
- Wear protective clothing.
- Avoid spraying during windy conditions.
- Keep children and livestock away during spraying.
- Observe recommended pre-harvest intervals.

---

# Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

The best results are achieved by combining several control methods rather than relying on pesticides alone.

Integrated Pest Management includes:

- Early planting
- Weekly scouting
- Intercropping
- Good crop nutrition
- Mechanical removal
- Botanical pesticides
- Responsible chemical control

---

# Key Messages for Farmers

- Inspect maize fields every week.
- Detect Fall Armyworm early.
- Plant maize early.
- Intercrop with legumes.
- Use Mthumba where appropriate.
- Apply approved pesticides only when necessary.
- Combine several control methods for the best results.
